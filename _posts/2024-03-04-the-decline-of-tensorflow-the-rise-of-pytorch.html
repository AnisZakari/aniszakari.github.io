TensorFlow was once essential, but I see fewer and fewer reasons to use it today. PyTorch seems to be establishing itself as the go-to deep learning framework.

TensorFlow from Google or PyTorch from Meta?

I was originally very partial to TensorFlow because it had a large community and was very popular. Moreover, Andrew Ng's essential deep learning course used this framework.

I was actually surprised to see him offer a generative AI course last year with AWS and Hugging Face - a direct competitor of Google and a major player in the PyTorch ecosystem. Andrew Ng no longer works for Google, but I still see this as a form of repudiation.

The fact is, the torch has set the flow on fire. The trend has reversed and the demand for PyTorch has surpassed that of TensorFlow on Stack Overflow.

PyTorch has several decisive advantages:

▶ Developed specifically for Python, it is more intuitive and "Pythonic", simpler to debug. While TensorFlow is more complex, with a higher barrier to entry.

▶ Its ease of use accelerated its adoption in research, followed by students and now industry.

▶ Hugging Face has greatly contributed to the craze around PyTorch. Today, nearly 90% of models in the hub are exclusive to PyTorch. Moreover, to my knowledge, there are no LLMs developed in TensorFlow.

The curve shows an acceleration of the decline starting in 2020, the year when:

  • Google DeepMind announced switching from TensorFlow to JAX, Google's new framework, to accelerate its development.

  • OpenAI standardized the use of PyTorch within its organization.

  • PyTorch facilitated large-scale model deployment via TorchServe, its equivalent of TensorFlow Serving.

And finally, the cornerstone of TensorFlow, which was Keras, can now be used with PyTorch.

Do you still see reasons to use TensorFlow?
